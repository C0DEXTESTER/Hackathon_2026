How the Analysis Works

From two PDFs to an interactive similarity report — step by step, entirely on your computer.

  1. 1

    Upload Two Papers

    You provide the reference paper (the original source) and the student paper (the submission to check). Both must be PDF files. Drag & drop or browse — nothing is uploaded to the internet.

  2. 2

    Text Extraction

    Each PDF is opened page by page and its text is extracted. Page numbers are remembered so the report can point you to exactly where matching text appears in both papers.

  3. 3

    Chunking

    Long papers are split into overlapping chunks (roughly paragraph-sized). Comparing chunks instead of whole documents makes the matches precise and localizable.

  4. 4

    AI Embeddings (Semantic)

    Every chunk is converted into a 384-dimensional meaning vector using a sentence-transformer model. Texts that mean the same thing end up close together in vector space — even if the wording is completely different. This is what catches paraphrasing.

  5. 5

    Lexical Matching

    Alongside the AI, a word-overlap method (TF-IDF cosine similarity) measures how much of the exact vocabulary is shared. This catches direct copy-paste that embeddings might score slightly lower.

  6. 6

    Scoring & Risk Levels

    Each student chunk is compared against every reference chunk. The best matches are combined into per-chunk scores (70% semantic + 30% lexical) and classified:

    High 75%+ Medium 50–75% Low <50%

  7. 7

    Interactive Report

    You get an overall similarity donut, breakdown bars, a distribution histogram and side-by-side matched text with page numbers — with filtering, sorting, export and print. Every analysis is saved to your local history.

Important: similarity scores are indicators, not verdicts. Quoted text, common terminology, methods sections and references can legitimately raise scores. Human review is always required before drawing conclusions.
